### Reliability depends on who was scored

Reliability is a ratio over the models actually scored on the benchmark, so a benchmark run only on a narrow slice of capability reads low however sharp it is.

## Is capability one number?

A posterior predictive check compares the mean squared correlation of residuals over 509 benchmark pairs (sharing at least 12 models) with the same statistic on tables simulated from the fit: observed 0.089, simulated 0.046 (95th percentile 0.051), p = 0.002.
On simulated tables the check rejects 1 of 40 without a second factor, 12 of 40 with a weak one (loading sd 0.3) and 39 of 40 with a strong one (0.8), so it is well calibrated but would often miss a weak second factor.
The audit pointed out that the most correlated residuals sit among older multiple-choice benchmarks with chance floors, which the model does not have. Refitting with a per-benchmark floor gives observed 0.088 against simulated 0.046 (p = 0.002).
The largest fitted floors: TriviaQA 0.50, LAMBADA 0.48, DeepResearch Bench 0.36, Lech Mazur Writing 0.26, GeoBench 0.26, Surface Evolver Bench 0.18.
The floor removes 1% of the excess residual correlation.
Leftover structure remains with floors, so part of it is not link misfit; the ratings are reliability with respect to one general factor.
Most correlated residual pairs with floors (exploratory): LAMBADA with PIQA (+0.84, 19 models); HellaSwag with PIQA (+0.81, 32 models); HellaSwag with LAMBADA (+0.78, 17 models); HellaSwag with TriviaQA (+0.75, 25 models); Fiction.LiveBench with SimpleQA Verified (+0.70, 13 models).

## What these results do not show

- That a benchmark measures what it says it measures. Reliability is internal consistency with the other benchmarks in the table.
- That the table is representative: it is the set Epoch fits its index on, already filtered to benchmarks that fit that index.
- That the simulation study validates coverage on real data: its generator is the fitted model, so its recovery numbers are a best case.
